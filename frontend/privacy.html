The status of this page

This has not been drafted or reviewed by a lawyer and is not a compliant privacy notice. What it is: an accurate description of what this site’s code actually collects and stores, written by reading the code, plus a list of what a real notice needs and this one has not got.

An inaccurate privacy policy is worse than an honest description, because it tells you that somebody checked when nobody did. If you need the compliant version — and for European customers High Route does — it has to be drafted against the real data flows, and this page is a useful starting inventory for that.

What is collected

Written from the database schema, so this is the actual list rather than a generic one:

If you create an account

  • Name, email address, phone number and country.
  • A password, stored as a salted hash — never the password itself.
  • Session tokens, stored as hashes, which expire.
  • Email verification status.

If you book

  • The departure, the riders on the booking and their details, and anything you entered as a special requirement — which may include dietary or medical information you choose to give.
  • Amounts, discounts, tax, invoices, and the last four digits of the card. No full card number, expiry or security code is stored.
  • Emergency contact details where you provide them.

If you send an enquiry

  • What you asked for: activity, destination, dates, group size, difficulty and accommodation preference, budget band, experience, special requirements, and a phone number if you give one.

Other

  • Newsletter: your email address, if you subscribe.
  • Adventure Finder: nothing at all unless you explicitly use the optional "save my matches" form, which stores your email address and the four answers. Using the finder itself stores nothing.
  • Reviews: your name as you give it, your scores and your comment.
  • During a trip, if location tracking is used: GPS positions against a departure.

What is not collected

Worth stating because it is unusual:

  • No analytics. No Google Analytics, no tag manager, no pixels, no heatmaps, no session recording.
  • No advertising or tracking cookies, and no third-party scripts beyond the two listed on the cookies page.
  • No full card details, ever.
  • No marketing consent assumed. The newsletter is opt-in and separate from booking.

Why, and on what basis

  • To run your booking — necessary to perform the contract.
  • To keep you safe on a trip — medical, dietary and emergency contact information, and location data where tracking is used.
  • To reply to an enquiry — you asked us to.
  • To send the newsletter — consent, withdrawable.
  • To keep financial records — legal obligation.

Where it is stored

In a SQLite database on the server that runs this site’s backend. The database file and its directory are restricted to the account running the server where the filesystem supports it. Passwords and session tokens are hashed.

To be confirmed: the hosting provider and the country the data physically sits in, retention periods for each category, backup locations, and who at High Route has access. For European customers the location and any transfer mechanism are the first things a compliant notice has to state, and none of them is settled yet.

Who else sees it

  • Your guide sees what they need for your departure — the rider list, dietary and medical notes, emergency contacts.
  • High Route administrators can see bookings, enquiries and accounts in the admin panel.
  • Nobody else through this site. There is no advertising network, no analytics provider and no data broker, because there is no analytics.

Trips in Nepal involve third parties in practice — lodges, permit authorities, domestic airlines, insurers — and some need passport or name details to issue a permit or a ticket. Which parties, for which trips, and on what terms is part of what the drafted notice has to set out.

Your rights

If you are in the UK or EU you have the right to see what is held about you, correct it, have it deleted, restrict or object to its processing, take it elsewhere, and withdraw consent.

In practice today: your dashboard shows your account, bookings and enquiries, and you can edit your profile there and cancel a booking. For anything beyond that — a full export, or deletion — email us and it is done by hand.

To be confirmed: a named data controller and contact point, a response time commitment, and the supervisory authority you could complain to. A rights section without a named controller is not actionable, which is why this is flagged rather than dressed up.

What is not settled

  • The legal identity of the data controller, its registered address and a contact point.
  • Hosting location, data-transfer mechanism, and retention periods per category.
  • The list of third-party processors and the terms they are engaged on.
  • A breach-notification procedure.
  • Legal review of the whole notice against UK GDPR and the EU GDPR.
