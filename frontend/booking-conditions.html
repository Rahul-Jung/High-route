The status of this page

This is not a professionally drafted or reviewed set of booking conditions, and it should not be relied on as one. It describes accurately how the booking system on this site behaves — which is checkable, because it is the same database the checkout runs against — and it flags the commercial and legal terms that High Route has to settle with a lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction before this page can be the contract.

Where a term below is described as how the system behaves, that is verifiable. Where it is a commercial policy, it is marked as to be confirmed rather than stated as settled.

Making a booking

You pick a departure date on an adventure page and go through rider details, any optional extras, and payment. An account is created as part of that, so you have somewhere to see the booking, the riders on it and the invoice afterwards.

A booking is confirmed when the system confirms it — not when the form submits. The booking is held while payment is outstanding and the status shown on your dashboard is the authoritative one. If anything on this site ever tells you a booking is confirmed when the system has not confirmed it, that is a defect.

Places are allocated when a booking is completed, and a departure shows the number of spaces actually remaining on it. There is no inflated scarcity anywhere on this site: if it says two spaces, there are two.

Payment and deposits

Each adventure shows its deposit on its dates and prices section, with the balance due before departure.

To be confirmed: the deposit figures currently in the system were set at 20% of trip price during a data migration and have not been confirmed by High Route. The amount shown at checkout is the authority, and every trip page carries this same caveat. Also to be confirmed: how long before departure the balance is due, and what happens if it is not paid.

An unpaid booking does not hold a place indefinitely. A new booking is held for 72 hours; if no payment is recorded in that window the hold expires on its own and the seats go back to the departure — you do not need to cancel anything. That is how a place becomes available again rather than being quietly held.

How payment is taken. Online card payment is not connected to this site yet, so nothing is charged online. We send payment details, and when the money arrives a member of the team records it against your booking; your dashboard shows the updated balance. The balance can be paid the same way, in one go or in parts, any time before departure.

Prices are per person in the currency shown on the trip. Anything compulsory but charged separately — permits, conservation-area fees — appears under mandatory charges on the trip page rather than being left to be discovered. Permit fees are set by Nepali government bodies and change, so the current rate is confirmed at booking rather than printed where it would go stale.

How a cancellation works in practice

  • You can cancel a booking that has not yet departed yourself, from your dashboard, or ask us to. The seats are released immediately.
  • Any refund on what you have already paid is calculated automatically from how many days before departure you cancelled, using the tiers above, and shown to you at the time.
  • A calculated refund is an entitlement, not a completed payment. It is recorded as pending until a member of the team has actually sent the money back and confirms it. Nothing is marked refunded before that happens.
  • Some trips carry costs that are genuinely non-refundable once committed — a restricted-area permit applied for weeks ahead is the usual example. Where that applies it is stated on that trip's own page. If a trip page does not mention one, none has been set; that is not a promise either way, so ask if it matters to your decision.
  • Tell us as soon as you know — the earlier you cancel, the higher the refund tier.

If we cancel

If a departure is cancelled you are offered an alternative date or a full refund of everything you have paid High Route.

That does not cover anything you booked separately — international flights above all — which is the main practical reason travel insurance with cancellation cover is a condition of booking rather than a suggestion.

To be confirmed: the minimum numbers below which a departure may be cancelled, and how long before departure that decision is taken. Every trip shows a minimum group size, and a departure that does not reach it is the usual reason a trip does not run — but the notice period is a commercial term High Route has to set.

Changes to an itinerary

Himalayan itineraries change. Snow closing a pass, a landslide across a road section, a cancelled mountain flight to Jomsom or Lukla: these are ordinary conditions and the plan is built with alternatives.

Your guide makes that call on the ground, and you will be told what changed and why. A change of this kind is not a downgrade of the trip and does not create a refund entitlement — the alternative is being taken over a 5,000 m pass in bad weather to protect a schedule, which no reputable operator will do.

Insurance

Travel insurance is a condition of booking. It must cover your activity, the maximum altitude of your trip, helicopter evacuation and repatriation. Many standard policies exclude activity above 3,000 m or 4,000 m, which rules out most trips on this site — read that clause in the policy wording. See Safety.

Your responsibilities

  • Give accurate information about fitness, experience and medical conditions. An itinerary is planned around the group, and a group is planned around what we were told about it.
  • Hold a valid passport and the correct visa. Entry requirements are set by Nepal’s Department of Immigration and are your responsibility to check.
  • Hold the insurance described above for the whole trip.
  • Follow your guide’s decisions on route, pace and turning back. These are safety decisions.
  • Bring the equipment listed as essential on your trip page.
