Why this exists

Nepal has been sold as a trekking destination for fifty years, and the infrastructure that grew up around that — the teahouses, the guides, the permit system, the flight network — turns out to support a great deal more than walking. Over the last two decades, road building through the Annapurna and Mustang valleys has put a continuous rideable line through terrain that used to take a fortnight on foot. That is a loss if you came to walk the old trail. It is an opportunity if you came with a bike.

High Route exists to run that properly: as a specialist operator, with Nepali guides, at a group size that the villages on the route can absorb — rather than as a mountain-bike line item bolted onto a trekking company’s brochure.

Why Nepal rewards specialists

Three things about this country make local knowledge worth more than it is in most places:

  • The weather decides, not the itinerary. A pass closes, a road slides, a flight to Jomsom or Lukla does not go. These are ordinary conditions rather than exceptions, and the difference between a good trip and a bad one is usually whether the person leading it has seen this particular weather in this particular valley before.
  • Altitude is unforgiving of a bad plan. An itinerary that gains height too fast will turn back fit people, and no amount of training fixes it. Building in the acclimatisation days costs money and loses bookings to operators who do not, which is exactly why it is worth asking about.
  • The route changes year to year. Road construction, landslides and rebuilding after the 2015 earthquake mean that a route description written three years ago may describe something that is no longer there. Routes here are confirmed close to departure for that reason.

How the trips are shaped

Four decisions that are structural rather than marketing, and which you can check by asking:

  • Small groups. Capped between 8 and 12 depending on the route, and lower on the remote circuits. A guide who can see everyone can make a judgement call for everyone — and a village of forty people and one teahouse cannot absorb twenty guests without the place changing for the people who live there.
  • Nepali guides and support team, from the regions they guide in, employed directly.
  • Village lodges and teahouses on the hill routes rather than operator-owned camps, so that the money for a bed goes to the village.
  • Difficulty stated in three dimensions rather than one, so that you can see whether it is the fitness, the technical ground or the altitude that will stop you. See the Difficulty Guide.

Beyond mountain biking

Mountain biking is the launch category and the only one with trips published today. The platform was built from the start to run trekking, rafting, multi-activity and trail running from the same model — one adventure record, one detail page, one difficulty framework, one set of filters — so that adding an activity is a content decision rather than a rebuild.

What that does not mean is that there are trekking trips to sell. There are not, and the trekking category says so rather than listing an invented itinerary. When it has real trips in it, they will appear there.

What we will and will not claim

There is a particular style of adventure-travel copy that this site tries not to write. A few specific commitments:

  • No invented availability. Departure dates and remaining spaces on this site come out of the database. A trip with no dates says it has no dates rather than showing a plausible one.
  • No borrowed reviews. Reviews are written by riders who rode that departure, from their own account afterwards. A trip with none says so.
  • No unverified qualifications. A certificate recorded in the admin panel does not appear on a guide’s public profile until somebody has checked it.
  • No sustainability figures nobody measured. The Our Impact page shows which figures are published and which are not, and the unpublished ones say what has to be supplied before they can be.
  • No safety procedure we have not been given. The Safety page is shorter than most operators’ for exactly that reason.

If you find something on this site that reads like a claim but turns out to be an assumption, that is a defect rather than a style choice, and we would like to hear about it — contact us.

What still has to be supplied

Being straight about the state of this site as well as the trips: the following are genuinely outstanding and are marked as missing wherever they would otherwise appear, rather than filled in with something plausible.

  • Company registration number, trading licence and registered address — which is why the footer does not show them.
  • Region-by-region emergency and evacuation procedure, insurance details and any safety certifications.
  • Deposit amounts. The figures currently in the system were set at 20% of trip price during a data migration and are flagged as placeholders on every trip page.
  • The operational impact figures described on the Our Impact page.
