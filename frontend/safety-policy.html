Safety policy

What we actually do to manage risk on a High Route MTB departure, and what this platform does and doesn't cover. Tour-specific hazards, preparation notes and emergency information live on each tour's own page — this is the baseline that applies to every departure.

Every departure includes

A wilderness-trained lead guide, a satellite communicator carried by the guide team, and a route that's been ridden and planned in advance — not improvised on the day. Guide profiles on each tour page list their years of experience, languages and any verified qualifications (for example a wilderness first-responder certification) — we only display a qualification once it's been checked, not just claimed.

Altitude

Several of our routes climb well above 3,000m, and our Himalayan-expedition-grade trips go higher still. Altitude-related illness is a real risk at these elevations regardless of fitness. Where a tour's itinerary includes an acclimatisation day, it's shown in that tour's day-by-day route section; where a tour's page notes altitude-specific preparation, read it before booking. If a tour page doesn't list a specific altitude hazard or preparation note, that means we haven't published one yet for that trip — ask us directly rather than assuming none applies.

Equipment

Each tour page lists the essential and recommended equipment for that specific trip where we've published it. Essential items are ones we expect every rider to have; recommended items are a genuine improvement to comfort or safety but not mandatory. A support vehicle or mechanic, where included, is listed in that tour's inclusions.

Route and GPS data

Where a tour's day-by-day itinerary includes recorded overnight-stop coordinates, we show them on that tour's route map. These are approximate positions for trip planning, not surveyed GPX data, and a tour with no recorded coordinates shows no map rather than an invented one. Live GPS tracking during a departure (see our tracker) is a demo/simulation feature in this build — it is not a substitute for a real satellite emergency communicator, which is what guides actually carry.

Emergency response

Our guides carry satellite communication and are trained to manage incidents on-route and coordinate evacuation with local support. This site's emergency-alert feature is a software demo of that workflow, not a connection to a real 24/7 operations desk or emergency service — a production deployment of this platform would need to be built out with qualified local safety and medical professionals, not shipped as software alone.

What we don't promise

Mountain biking in remote, high-altitude terrain carries inherent risk that no policy or piece of equipment eliminates. We don't claim a specific hazard has been assessed for a tour unless that tour's own safety-information section says so. If you have a medical condition, are unsure about the altitude or technical demands of a route, or want detail beyond what's published, contact us before booking rather than assuming.
